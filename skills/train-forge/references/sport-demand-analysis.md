# Sport-demand analysis

Use this framework for every sport-performance request. Sport categories below are analytical aids, never a whitelist.

## 1. Define the exact sport problem

Identify:

- Sport and exact discipline or event
- Position, role, weight class, apparatus, boat/crew role, stroke, distance, surface, or ruleset where applicable
- Recreational, developmental, competitive, elite, or return-to-participation context
- Current season stage and next meaningful event/date
- Current technical/tactical sessions, competition frequency, coaching access, and selection criteria
- The performance result the athlete actually wants to change

Examples of meaningful specificity: goalkeeper rather than football; 100 m freestyle rather than swimming; singles badminton rather than racket sport; Olympic recurve rather than archery; road cycling criterium rather than cycling.

## 2. Verify the event environment

For unfamiliar, regulated, high-risk, or current-rule-dependent activities, verify authoritative sources before prescribing. Establish:

- Event duration, rounds/heats, substitutions, rest intervals, attempts, scoring, and competition density
- Field/court/track/pool/water/snow/ice/road/indoor conditions
- Equipment, protective gear, footwear, implement mass, and relevant rules
- Weight-class, weigh-in, travel, altitude, heat, cold, or hydration constraints

Do not treat a competition rule, equipment standard, or event format as timeless when it may change.

## 3. Build the demand matrix

| Domain | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Skill and tactics | Which decisions, techniques, timing, accuracy, perception, and partner/opponent interactions decide performance? |
| Movement | Which locomotion, stance, rotation, jumping, landing, throwing, striking, pulling, pushing, carrying, balance, or apparatus patterns dominate? |
| Force–velocity | Is the need maximal force, rate of force development, ballistic power, acceleration, maximal velocity, deceleration, or repeated actions? |
| Energy and duration | What are the typical effort durations, recovery periods, total event time, and repeatability demands? |
| Tissue load | Which joints, tendons, muscles, impact/contact exposures, or repetitive patterns receive the highest load? |
| Range and coordination | Which mobility, stability, rhythm, balance, precision, or coordination limits technique? |
| Body composition | Does mass, lean mass, power-to-weight ratio, buoyancy, weight class, or aerodynamic profile matter—and how much? |
| Environment | What surface, weather, altitude, water, temperature, travel, or equipment changes the physical demand? |
| Calendar | How many practices, matches, races, attempts, and travel days constrain training? |

Use more than one energy-system label. Most sports combine metabolic, neuromuscular, technical, and tactical fatigue.

## 4. Translate demands into trainable qualities

Rank each as primary, secondary, maintenance, or irrelevant for the current block:

- Technical/tactical quality
- Maximal strength
- Hypertrophy or tissue capacity
- Explosive power and reactive strength
- Acceleration and maximal speed
- Change of direction and reactive agility
- Aerobic capacity and recovery
- Threshold or sustainable event pace
- Anaerobic power and repeated high-intensity ability
- Mobility, stability, balance, coordination, and robustness
- Body-composition or weight-management objective
- Psychological routine, pacing, and competition rehearsal where within scope

Select tests that match these ranked qualities. Avoid testing metrics that will not change decisions.

## 5. Respect specificity and transfer

- Let sport practice develop sport skill, perception, timing, and tactics.
- Use physical training to increase the underlying capacity that practice cannot safely or efficiently provide.
- Prefer exercises with the required force direction, contraction type, velocity intent, range, and tissue tolerance without mimicking the sport superficially.
- Do not turn every gym movement into an unstable or sport-shaped imitation.
- Do not assume a larger gym number transfers if body mass, skill, fatigue, or event economy worsens.

## 6. Place the athlete in the calendar

| Phase | Primary physical-training aim |
| --- | --- |
| General preparation | Build broad capacity, tissue tolerance, strength, and aerobic base |
| Specific preparation | Shift toward sport-relevant velocity, power, event pace, repeatability, and competition rehearsal |
| Pre-competition | Preserve strength, reduce fatigue, increase specificity, practice event routines |
| Competition | Maintain key qualities with minimum dose around events |
| Taper/peaking | Reduce volume while retaining enough intensity and specificity |
| Transition | Recover physically and psychologically; maintain light general activity |

Do not force this terminology on recreational users; still account for busy periods, holidays, and informal events.

## 7. Organize weekly stress

- Place demanding strength, speed, power, intervals, and hard technical sessions so their fatigue does not repeatedly collide.
- Consolidate compatible high-stress work on the same day when this creates clearer recovery days.
- Protect the freshest state for the highest-priority technical, speed, or power work.
- In team sports, organize around practices, matches, travel, and role-specific exposure.
- In endurance sports, coordinate strength with key long, threshold, race-pace, and high-intensity sessions.
- In skill/precision sports, manage local tissue load, postural fatigue, attention, and repetition quality even when metabolic demand is modest.
- In judged, acrobatic, contact, combat, aquatic, outdoor, and high-speed sports, keep appropriate in-person coaching and safety systems.

## 8. Specialist boundaries

Ask for or defer to a qualified sport coach when technique, spotting, water/open-terrain safety, collision/contact management, apparatus, weapons, animals, vehicles, altitude, heat, return-to-play, or competition rules exceed remote programming scope.

Provide physical preparation around those boundaries instead of inventing detailed technical instruction.
