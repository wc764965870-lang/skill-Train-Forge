# Intake and safety framework

Use this reference during the health-screening stage of `intake-flow.md`, and whenever health or availability materially changes. Ask 1–3 concise questions per turn and reuse every answer already known.

## Contents

- High-value intake data
- Efficient interview sequence
- Performance anchors
- Medical report handling
- Pause/clearance conditions
- Population modifiers

## High-value data to request when relevant

| Domain | Information that may improve the plan | Why it changes the plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sport / goal | Exact sport, discipline, event, position/role or personal physical goal; one primary outcome; target date | Defines the real performance problem |
| Identity | Age; height; weight; relevant sex, pregnancy, or postpartum context | Helps dose recovery and identify special considerations |
| Experience | Training age, sport history, current technical/tactical and physical workload, technique confidence | Determines complexity and starting volume |
| Performance | Goal-specific anchors such as working weights, repetitions, pace, rounds, grades, or duration | Calibrates the starting level |
| Logistics | Days per week, minutes per session, equipment, surface/environment, location, coach/partner access, competition/travel calendar | Defines the real schedule |
| Health | Current pain, significant injury or surgery, diagnosed condition, medication effects, clinician restrictions | Determines safety and substitutions |
| Recovery | Sleep, work/study stress, daily steps/activity, recent fatigue | Limits total recoverable load |
| Adherence | Preferred and disliked activities, previous plan failures, non-negotiable commitments | Makes the plan repeatable |

Do not require precise body-fat data. Home scales are noisy; use waist, trend weight, photos under consistent conditions, and performance when relevant.

None of these items is automatically mandatory. Offer “不知道 / 暂不回答 / 没有这项资料”. Ask the highest-impact missing item first, stop when the user wants the plan, and expose the resulting assumptions and precision level.

## Efficient interview sequence

### Round 1 — direction

Invite answers to:

1. The exact sport/discipline/event/position or physical goal, current season stage, and next important date.
2. Current technical practice, competition, physical training, and experience.
3. Available training days, session length, facilities, equipment, coaching, and travel constraints.

### Round 2 — person and safety

Invite answers to:

1. Age, height, weight, and relevant sex/pregnancy context.
2. Current pain, injury/surgery history, diagnosed conditions, medication effects, and restrictions.
3. Two or three sport- or goal-specific performance anchors.

### Round 3 — calibration

Invite only if it would materially improve the plan:

- Sleep and stress
- Typical daily activity
- Body-weight/waist trend
- Exercise preferences and dislikes
- Competition, travel, shift work, fasting, or recovery constraints
- More precise training records or video/form observations

## Performance anchors

Choose only anchors that change the program:

| Demand | Useful anchors |
| --- | --- |
| Competition outcome | Time, distance, score, ranking, grade, accuracy, successful attempts, role-specific statistics, judged routine, or event completion |
| Technical/tactical load | Sessions and minutes/week, drill/live-play split, rounds/heats/attempts, match/race calendar, coach assessment |
| Strength | Stable work sets, submaximal repetition performance, isometric output, or sport-specific strength test when valid |
| Speed/power | Sprint splits, jump/throw output, bar velocity, acceleration, change-of-direction, implement speed, or repeated-output decay |
| Endurance | Easy duration, time trial, pace/power, threshold marker, stroke/cadence, repeat quality, or recovery between efforts |
| Mobility/coordination | A specific range, balance, control, or movement limitation that affects technique or symptoms |
| Body composition | Seven-day average weight, waist/composition trend, weight class, power-to-weight need, strength/performance trend |
| General function | Walking/stair tolerance, sit-to-stand, push/pull capacity, balance confidence, daily activity |

For an unfamiliar sport, ask what the coach or athlete already measures and verify the event format before inventing a test.

## Medical report handling

When a report is provided:

1. Identify its date and whether it reflects the person's current state.
2. Extract each relevant value with units and the laboratory or clinic reference range.
3. Separate clinician-diagnosed conditions from isolated out-of-range values.
4. Ask whether a clinician imposed exercise limits or requested follow-up.
5. Modify training only where the finding has a clear exercise implication.
6. Avoid diagnosing, predicting disease, or recommending medication changes.

Examples of potentially relevant data include blood pressure, resting ECG interpretation, glucose control, hemoglobin, lipids, liver/kidney markers, musculoskeletal imaging, pulmonary testing, and physician notes. Their meaning depends on context; do not convert a single number into a training clearance decision.

A medical report is optional. “No report”, “not sure”, and “prefer not to share” are complete answers; do not request the report again unless the user later asks for medical-report-aware adjustment.

## Pause and seek appropriate evaluation

Do not start or continue a hard plan when the person reports an unexplained or uncontrolled issue such as:

- Chest pain/pressure, fainting, or near-fainting during activity
- New neurological symptoms, severe headache, or symptoms after head impact
- Unusual severe breathlessness or palpitations
- Known cardiovascular, pulmonary, metabolic, or renal condition without clarity on exercise limits
- Very high or uncontrolled blood pressure reported by a clinician or repeated valid measurements
- Acute injury, rapidly increasing swelling, inability to bear weight, or worsening night pain
- Recent surgery without return-to-exercise guidance
- Pregnancy/postpartum symptoms or restrictions requiring obstetric/pelvic-health guidance
- Fever, systemic illness, or medication effects that make exercise unsafe

Recommend the appropriate clinician, physiotherapist, or qualified in-person coach rather than attempting a remote diagnosis. Emergency symptoms require urgent local care.

## Population modifiers

- **Adolescents:** prioritize supervision, technique, enjoyment, broad athletic skills, and adequate nutrition; avoid aggressive dieting and routine maximal testing.
- **Older adults:** begin conservatively; include strength, aerobic work, balance, and appropriately scaled power; allow more recovery when needed.
- **Pregnancy/postpartum:** use clinician guidance, symptoms, training history, and trimester/postpartum stage; avoid generic one-size-fits-all rules.
- **Higher body weight or low conditioning:** favor supported and low-impact modes, shorter initial bouts, and gradual volume progression.
- **Women and menstrual-cycle context:** use reported symptoms and performance rather than automatically changing training by calendar phase.
- **Disability or chronic condition:** ask what movements, equipment, and assistance are effective; design around capability rather than assumptions.
