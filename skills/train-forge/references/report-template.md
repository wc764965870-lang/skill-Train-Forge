# Sport and personal training report template

Use this structure for every complete plan. Adjust the labels to the user's language.

## Contents

- Athlete dashboard
- Demand and priority allocation
- Weekly course table and session cards
- Load and progression guide
- Phase timeline and readiness
- Performance dashboard and substitutions
- Visual webpage standard

## 1. Athlete dashboard

Show only decision-relevant metrics:

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Participant route | Competitive/professional, recreational sport, or fitness-only |
| Sport / event | Exact discipline, event, position/role; use “fitness-only” when there is no primary sport |
| Level and phase | Training experience; add competition level and season phase only when relevant |
| Primary goal | One clear outcome for the block |
| Secondary goal | Optional, with lower priority |
| Calendar | Block length, target date, competitions/travel, and review date |
| Baseline | Experience and 2–5 goal-specific performance anchors |
| Existing load | Technical/tactical practice, competition, and physical training |
| Schedule | Available days/week and minutes/session |
| Constraints | Equipment, pain, medical restrictions, travel, dislikes |
| Screening | Green, yellow, red, or not assessed; explain limitations without claiming clearance |
| Data precision | Basic, standard, or enhanced |
| Unknown / declined | Only items that materially affect interpretation; do not frame optional data as failure |

## 2. Goal and demand allocation

For fitness-only users, rank health, adherence, muscle, strength, body composition, conditioning, mobility/balance, or function as primary, secondary, maintenance, or not targeted. For sport participants, add the sport-demand analysis and technical/tactical priorities. Percentages express emphasis, not literal time accounting; ensure the total is 100% when used.

## 3. Advanced weekly course table

| Day | Course | Type | Duration | Intensity | Primary task | Completion standard |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |

Label strength/hypertrophy, aerobic/conditioning, mobility/balance, technical/tactical practice, competition, daily activity, recovery, and rest as relevant. Include recovery days; do not hide rest behind optional workouts.

## 4. Detailed session cards

For each training day:

| Time | Module | Exercise / drill / dose | Starting load or output | Rest | Technique / stop standard |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- |

Include:

- Warm-up that prepares the session rather than causing fatigue
- Primary work
- Secondary work
- Clearly marked optional work
- Cool-down only where useful
- RPE/RIR or talk-test target
- One concise technique cue per primary movement
- Explicit assumptions and session-1 calibration tasks when data is sparse

Starting load may be a KG range, machine-specific calibration, body weight, implement mass, resistance, distance, pace, power, heart-rate zone, grade, time, contacts, attempts, or competition intensity. Mark unknown values as “calibrate in session 1,” not as zero.

## 5. Load and progression guide

Explain:

- Unit conventions for KG, distance, duration, pace, power, speed, grade, resistance, contacts, attempts, and assistance
- What earns more load, repetitions, rounds, duration, distance, pace, power, complexity, attempts, or difficulty
- The smallest practical progression step
- What forces a reduction
- When to test, and when not to test

## 6. Phase timeline

Build the timeline from the season and target date. A general 12-week preparation block without frequent competition may use:

| Weeks | Phase | Load strategy | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–3 | Foundation | Calibrate and accumulate clean work | Consistency and technique |
| 4 | Recovery | Reduce volume about 25%–40% as needed | Absorb training |
| 5–7 | Build | Resume and progress one variable | Capacity and performance |
| 8 | Recovery | Reduce fatigue | Prepare to consolidate |
| 9–11 | Consolidate | Continue measured progression | Stable output |
| 12 | Assessment | Use submaximal, goal-specific tests | Set the next block |

Do not force this pattern over matches, races, tournaments, performance dates, or tapers. Show competition, travel, taper/peak, and transition weeks where relevant. A shorter 8-week general block can use three build weeks, one lighter week, three build weeks, and one assessment week.

## 7. Readiness traffic light

| Status | Typical signs | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Green | Normal sleep, no concerning pain, warm-up feels normal | Complete the plan |
| Yellow | Repeated poor sleep, unusual heaviness, persistent soreness, performance down about 5%–10% | Remove optional work; reduce compound sets or intensity; keep extra RIR |
| Red | Sharp/worsening pain, chest symptoms, fainting, neurological symptoms, unusual severe breathlessness, or symptoms after head impact | Stop and seek appropriate evaluation |

Adapt yellow rules to the sport: remove the lowest-priority volume, extra attempts, intervals, accessories, or contact exposure before automatically removing high-value technical work.

## 8. Progress dashboard

Track 4–8 measures, not everything available:

| Category | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Sport result | Time, score, distance, ranking, accuracy, successful attempts, coach-rated execution, or role-specific statistic |
| Technical/tactical | Practice quality, error pattern, decision quality, routine consistency, or execution under fatigue |
| Body | Seven-day average weight, waist, symptom trend |
| Strength/hypertrophy | Stable work sets, repetitions, estimated RIR, circumference or photos |
| Speed/power | Sprint split, jump/throw output, bar/implement speed, repeated-output decay |
| Endurance | Easy duration, pace/power at comparable effort, threshold/event-pace output, interval repeat quality |
| Recovery | Sleep, readiness, pain, adherence |

State the review trigger. Default: adjust structure after two consistent weeks; respond to safety issues immediately.

When starting from basic-precision data, schedule an early review after the first 1–2 weeks so actual loads, tolerance, attendance, and preferences can replace initial assumptions.

## 9. Substitution table

Every substitution must preserve the training slot:

| Slot | Original | Substitute | When to use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Replace; do not add. Keep equipment, pain, skill, and goal in view.

## Visual webpage standard

When a webpage is requested, reuse the stored boxing report's information hierarchy while adapting the content and visual language to the sport:

- A compact athlete dashboard in the first viewport
- Clickable weekly day cards
- A selected-day timed session card
- Visible KG/load/output tags appropriate to the sport
- Phase timeline, readiness controls, and progress paths
- Responsive desktop/mobile layout and print-friendly styling
- Exact selectable text; no screenshot-only timetable

Use a focused performance-dashboard visual style appropriate to the activity. Preserve readability and do not turn the report into a decorative landing page.
